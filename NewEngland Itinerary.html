~3:00 PM

Norman Rockwell Museum

This is the trip's first and most anticipated stop. The museum holds the world's largest collection of Rockwell's original art — 998 paintings, drawings, and studies. The "Four Freedoms" series is here. His actual studio was moved to the museum grounds and is preserved exactly as he left it the day he died in 1978, right down to the coffee mug. The museum sits on a hillside with views that look like a Rockwell painting itself. Plan 2–3 hours. Last entry is typically 4:30 PM in summer — aim to arrive no later than 3:30.

Must-See #1
Before Dinner

Stroll Stockbridge Main Street

This is the literal street Norman Rockwell painted for his famous 1967 "Stockbridge Main Street at Christmas." The Red Lion Inn anchors the corner. The unchanged storefronts, white-steeple churches, and elm-lined sidewalks make it feel timeless. A walk before dinner is the perfect way to arrive.

Explore
Dinner

Red Lion Inn Dining Room

Fine linens, candlelight, a James Beard House-honored kitchen — exactly right for night one. Widow Bingham's Tavern downstairs is cozier and more casual if you'd prefer. Both use the same kitchen. The award-winning wine list is excellent. After dinner: cocktails on the famous front porch with rocking chairs.

The Red Lion Porch

The iconic front porch with rocking chairs overlooking Main Street is the most coveted seat in Stockbridge. Presidents have sat here. Rockwell painted it. Don't skip it.

Take US-20 through Pittsfield and Hancock rather than the highway for a more scenic route through the Berkshire foothills and into the Hudson Valley. Albany sits at the confluence of the Hudson and Mohawk Rivers — one of the oldest continuously settled European communities in America.

Explore Washington Park & Center Square

Washington Park is a 90-acre Victorian park designed by Frederick Law Olmsted's firm — the same team that designed Central Park. The lake, rose gardens, and period lamp posts are gorgeous in summer. Center Square neighborhood surrounding the park has some of Albany's finest 19th-century row houses and is very walkable.

Albany's restaurant corridor. Top picks: Café Capriccio (beloved institution since 1981, outstanding Italian, James Beard-recognized) or Chez Mike (farm-to-table, excellent wine list). Mark Twain House & Museum — Hartford

One of America's greatest literary landmarks. The ornate Victorian Gothic mansion at 351 Farmington Ave was Twain's home from 1874–1891, and where he wrote Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, Tom Sawyer, The Prince and the Pauper, and A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court. Guided interior tours are outstanding — the house is full of Twain's wit and personality, right down to the billiard room where he entertained guests until all hours. The neighboring Harriet Beecher Stowe House is on the same property if you have time. Buy tickets at marktwainhouse.org before arriving — summer weekend tours sell out.

Must-See · Buy tickets in advance

The Parkville neighborhood near Farmington Ave has several solid lunch spots. Tisane Euro-Asian Café on Farmington Ave is excellent for a quick, quality stop. Alternatively, grab something light and hit the road — you have ground to cover.

The lobster dinner is a worthy pre-Maine warm-up. This is the kind of place locals have been bringing family for decades.

The Maine Coast Begins

Cross the Piscataqua River bridge into Maine and you'll know it immediately — the light changes, the pines crowd the highway, and the air smells different. Pull over at the Maine Welcome Center in Kittery for a stretch and the best free blueberry pie opportunity in the Northeast (they really do this). Follow I-95 N to Portland, then Route 1 or Route 3 north toward Ellsworth and the causeway to Mount Desert Island.

Scenic drive — pull over when it calls you

Optional: Lunch Stop — Coastal Route 1

If you're taking coastal Route 1 north of Portland, Red's Eats in Wiscasset is legendary for lobster rolls (expect a line). Or stop in Damariscotta for a quiet riverside lunch. If you're on the faster inland route, save the appetite for dinner in Southwest Harbor.

Arrive Southwest Harbor — Check In

9 Dirigo Road. Harbour Cottage Inn is a beautifully restored Victorian property right at the harbor. Your room is the Yawl — named for a sailboat rig, fitting for a property this close to the water. Breakfast is included each morning. The innkeepers are exceptional and will give you the best local advice you'll get anywhere on the island. Call ahead: (207) 244-5738.

Acadia — Your Pace, Your Day

Southwest Harbor & Acadia National Park
🏞️ No long drives today — all within Acadia & MDI

📍 Overnight: Harbour Cottage Inn (Night 2)

Day Schedule — Flexible Acadia Day

Morning

Breakfast at Harbour Cottage Inn

Take your time. Included breakfast, exceptional innkeepers who will adjust your plan based on the day's weather, tides, and crowds. This is one of the best mornings of the trip.

Included
Mid-Morning

Jordan Pond — Popovers & the View

The crown jewel of Acadia's south side. A glacier-carved pond reflecting the Bubbles — two symmetrical rounded mountains — is one of the most stunning natural scenes in the Northeast. The Jordan Pond House has served popovers with jam and tea for 130+ years; it's a genuine institution. The shoreline path around the pond is flat, wide, and remarkably easy walking on the gravel side. Total loop is 3.3 miles but the view from the near shore is just as good for a shorter stroll.

Must-See · Easy terrainJordan Pond House popovers
Early Afternoon

Bass Harbor Head Lighthouse

One of the most photographed lighthouses in America — a classic white-and-red structure perched on dramatic pink granite ledges above crashing surf on Acadia's southern tip. The setting is genuinely spectacular. The access path is short (about 5 minutes) with some uneven granite steps, but nothing difficult. Golden hour and blue hour photography here is extraordinary.

Must-See · Top photo stop
Afternoon

Thunder Hole — Conditions Permitting

A narrow Atlantic Ocean inlet on the Ocean Path where waves compress into an explosive surge and boom that can send spray 40 feet in the air. Best at 3/4 incoming tide with moderate surf. The innkeepers will know if conditions are right. It's a short, easy drive and walk — worth checking even if conditions are uncertain.

Optional — check tide conditions
~7:00 PM

Seawall Sunset — The Evening Plan

The Seawall picnic area on Acadia's western shore is the trip's most deliberate moment. Flat, easy access to the rocky shoreline. West-facing — one of the rare Maine spots where the sun actually sets over the water. Bring a blanket, a bottle of wine from town, and settle in. Sunset is around 8:35 PM in July. The light is long and golden and unforgettable. This is why you chose Southwest Harbor.

The evening plan — don't miss

Included
Mid-Morning

Fort Williams Park — Portland Head Light

George Washington commissioned this lighthouse in 1787 — it's the oldest in Maine and among the most photographed in America. The setting at Fort Williams Park is spectacular: the lighthouse on a granite headland, the Atlantic crashing on three sides, the skyline of Portland across the bay. The park grounds are extensive and free. Easy paved walking paths along the headland. The view north to Casco Bay is outstanding.

Must-See — one of America's finest lighthouses
Lunch

Bite Into Maine — The Lobster Roll

The food truck right at Fort Williams Park. This is one of Maine's most beloved lobster rolls — choose your style: Maine (cold, mayo) or Connecticut (warm, butter). The Connecticut-style is outstanding. Get a bag of chips and eat on the headland overlooking the lighthouse and the sea. This is a top-five trip moment. Cash accepted; line forms fast — arrive before noon if possible.

Bucket list food momentDining Priority — Arrive early
Early Afternoon

Salem, MA — Optional Stop (~30 min off I-95)

If the day's timing works, Salem is 25 miles north of Boston and worth 1.5–2 hours. The Salem Witch Trials Memorial (1692), the Peabody Essex Museum (world-class maritime art and Asian art collections), and Pickering Wharf are all within easy walking. Essex Street Pedestrian Mall is charming. Best visited late afternoon when day-trip crowds thin. Skip if you're tired — it's entirely optional.

Optional — depends on energy and timing
Evening

Boston — Copley Square & Dinner with Skadz

47 Huntington Ave puts you directly on Copley Square — Trinity Church, the Boston Public Library, the Hancock Tower. Check in, freshen up. The evening belongs to Skadz, who knows Boston's restaurant scene well. Back Bay and South End are extraordinary for dinner. Let him lead — trust the local.
